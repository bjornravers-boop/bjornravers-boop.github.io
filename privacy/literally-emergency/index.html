Privacy policy

Your location

With your permission, the app reads your location through Google Play services while the emergency screen is open, and stops when you leave it. Your coordinates are shown on screen and are never sent to our servers. To show a street address, the app uses Android’s built-in geocoder, which on most phones sends the coordinates to Google to look up the address. The Plus Code shown with it is calculated on the phone. If you use Silent SMS or “Text my emergency contacts”, your location is added to the message, which is sent only when you press send in your messaging app. When you search for a home address in My info, what you type is sent to the same geocoder to suggest matching addresses. Tapping an address on the info card opens it in Google Maps, which handles it under Google’s privacy policy.

My info

Details you enter in My info (for example your name, allergies, medication and emergency contacts) are stored only on this phone. They are excluded from Google backups and phone-to-phone transfers, and are never sent to us. The app shows them on screen after you start an emergency call; a phone call cannot carry them to the dispatcher. They are added to Silent SMS and Share location only if you switch that on, and are sent only when you press send. Use “Delete my info” or clear the app’s data to remove them. When you choose an emergency contact from your address book, Android gives the app only that contact’s name and number; the app cannot read your other contacts.

Lock screen, Quick Settings and printed card

If you switch on “Show medical info on the lock screen”, the app shows a silent notification with blood type, allergies, medication, conditions and one emergency contact. Tapping it, even while the phone is locked, also shows the name, date of birth, language, notes and all emergency contacts of that person (not the home address or card photos). It is visible to anyone who picks up your phone, which is the point; switch it off in Preferences at any time. The Quick Settings tile only opens your dialer with the emergency number. The printable card is a PDF made on the phone, with a QR code that holds the same text; it goes only where you share or save it.

Wear OS watch

If you install the watch app, the phone sends it the emergency numbers for where you are and, from My info, your name, blood type, allergies, medication, conditions, language and emergency contacts. They go through Google Play services' Wearable Data Layer, over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi and encrypted, from your phone to your own watch; never to our servers. Nothing is sent unless a watch with the app is paired. The watch keeps the last copy so it works without the phone; change it in My info, or remove it by uninstalling the watch app or clearing its data. Calls from the watch go through its dialer, where you press call yourself.

Card photos

You can photograph cards such as health insurance or hospital cards. The camera is used only while you scan a card; photos are cropped to the card and saved in the app’s private storage, excluded from backups, and shown on the info card. They are included in a family export only when you export that person, inside the same (password-protected) file. The share button in the card viewer makes a PDF of the card, which goes only where you share or save it; a PDF of a protected card has no password. Deleting a card or person deletes its photos.

Protected cards

You can give a card its own password. Its photos are then stored encrypted (AES-256), shown only as a tiny blurred preview, and opened only after that card’s password or, if you turn it on for that card, your fingerprint. Passwords are never stored; your fingerprint never leaves Android’s secure hardware, and the app only learns that it matched. Fingerprint unlock turns off by itself when fingerprints on the phone change. Screenshots are blocked while a protected card is open, and it locks again when you close it. In a family export a protected card stays encrypted with its own password. A forgotten password can’t be recovered; the card has to be scanned again.

Non-urgent numbers

The non-urgent numbers (doctor or pharmacy on call, and similar) are a list built into the app; showing them sends nothing anywhere. Numbers you add yourself are stored only on this phone and excluded from backups. Calls go through your phone’s dialer, where you press call yourself.

Translation

The info card can be translated with Google ML Kit, which runs on this phone: the text you entered is never sent to a translation service. The first time you use a language, the app downloads its translation model (about 30 MB) from Google; this download does not include your information.

Sharing with family

Export creates a .lecard file with the people you choose, which you send with an app of your choice (for example a messenger or email); that app handles the file under its own privacy policy. With a password (recommended), the file is encrypted with AES-256 and can only be opened with that password. Without one, anyone who gets the file can read it. The app never uploads these files itself; importing only reads the file you open.

Phone state

The phone permission is used to read the list of emergency numbers your network and SIM provide, and, only if you turn on emergency call notices (see below), to see that a call started. The app does not read your phone number, contacts or call history, and it never places a call itself: it opens your dialer and you decide whether to call.

Ring through silent

This is off until you turn it on in Preferences. If you make the app your caller ID app, Android shows it the number of each incoming call as it arrives. The app only compares it with the people you chose to always ring for, turns the ringer up if it matches, and lets every call through unchanged. Numbers are not stored or sent anywhere. With Do Not Disturb access the app turns the ringer up for those calls and puts your own settings back afterwards.

Emergency alarms between paired phones use the internet. To receive them, your phone gets a push token from Google’s Firebase Cloud Messaging, and we store that token on our alarm service (hosted by Cloudflare) under a random key, with no name or number. When a paired person calls you from the app, their phone sends a signed alarm to our service, which passes it through Firebase to your phone. An alarm holds only a random code, the time and a signature; your name, number and other info are never sent. The pairing codes and the people you paired with stay on this phone and are excluded from backups. Turning the feature off deletes the push token; the stored copy then no longer works.

To pair, one phone shows a code (as a QR code or a link you send with an app of your choice) and the other uses it. The phone that uses it answers through our alarm service with a message encrypted with the code’s secret, which only the two phones can read: it lets the phones alarm each other and replaces the secret, so each code works once, and only for 24 hours. A link opens a page on our alarm service that hands the code to the app; the code is in the part of the link after “#”, which browsers never send to a server, so it doesn’t reach us. Anyone who gets hold of an unused link or code could pair with you, so send it only to the person it’s for; you get a notification when it’s used. When you pair with one of your emergency contacts, the app links the two by phone number on your phone only.

From the info card you can send a paired person an emergency message: “I’m in an emergency” with your location, or words of your own, with your location if you choose. It is encrypted on your phone with the secret your two phones share and only their phone can read it; our alarm service and Google’s Firebase pass it on without being able to. It includes your location (coordinates, accuracy and address) only when shown in the message screen.

After an emergency message that includes your location, the app keeps sending your location to the same people about once a minute for 30 minutes, the same way, so they can find you. A notification shows this while it happens; tap “Stop sharing” to end it sooner. When someone taps “I’m coming” or “Call” on your message, their phone sends you that answer, the same way.

The check-in timer (off unless you start it) keeps your last known location on your phone while it runs. If you don’t confirm in time and don’t answer its alarm, your paired people get an emergency message with that location and the time it was taken. Stopping the timer deletes it.

If you switch on “Tell them when I call emergency services”, the app watches the phone’s call state for a few minutes after it opens the dialer with an emergency number. It sees only that a call started, not who you called. If a call starts, each person you paired with gets a notice through the same alarm service, holding the service and number you called (for example Ambulance, 112), the time and a signature. Nothing is sent if you don’t press call.

Optional advertising

The Android app uses Google AdMob for ads that you choose to watch, and Google’s User Messaging Platform to manage advertising privacy choices. These services may process your IP address (including approximate location), device or advertising identifiers, ad interactions and diagnostic information for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. Consent-related requests happen only when you open Support; ads are requested when you choose to watch one. The emergency buttons never require watching an ad.

Where applicable, you can change consent using “Ad privacy choices” in Support. You can also manage your advertising ID in Android settings. Google describes its data handling at Google Privacy Policy.

Optional donations

Donations open PayPal. PayPal handles your payment information under its own privacy policy. The app does not receive your card or bank details and stores only a local flag to ask whether you completed the donation. We may receive donor and transaction details through PayPal to manage donations.

Settings and reminders

The app stores a few settings on the phone: your home screen layout, whether the first-launch setup was done, and when My info was last edited or confirmed, so it can ask once a year whether it’s still right. None of this leaves the phone.

Accounts, retention and choices

No account or email address is required. My info, your digital-cookie collection count and the donation reminder are stored locally. Digital cookies are in-app collectibles, not browser tracking cookies. Location is kept in memory only and is not stored. Clearing app data removes everything. Advertising and payment providers manage their own retention. If you contact us by email, we use your message and address to respond and retain correspondence as needed to handle your request. “Give feedback” only opens your email app with a message you wrote; if you leave “Add phone details” on, it adds the app version, Android version and phone model. You decide whether to send it.
